# Present Health MD 2026 Strategy

Date: May 15, 2026 (Rev. 3 - founder-clinician spine, risk-hardened)

## Executive Thesis

The destination is a sustainable, founder-clinician-owned virtual primary care practice that you can eventually run full-time as owner-operator.

The model is direct cash-pay membership. The launch state is Michigan. The first version is deliberately small: one physician, one state, one clinical chart, one acquisition channel, and a tight adult cardiometabolic primary care scope.

The path is:

- Keep CenterWell as the W-2 income base.
- Build the practice on evenings and weekends only after written COI clearance.
- Do not do advisory or consulting as the active 2026 plan.
- Do not bill insurance.
- Do not sell employer PEPM.
- Do not launch multi-state.
- Leave CenterWell only after the practice shows sustained replacement economics, operational stability, and personal cash reserves.

The brand stays Present Health MD. The legacy DPC naming should stay historical. The practice can use "direct primary care" or "direct virtual primary care," but the public promise must be simple: relationship-based virtual primary care with a real family physician, cash-pay, no insurance friction.

The strongest constructive criticism of this plan: it is slower and riskier than advisory-first cashflow. It sacrifices near-term consulting income to build the owned practice. That is acceptable only if the plan stays brutally narrow and milestone-driven.

## The Hard Gate: COI

Do not assume COI clearance operationally. Plan for a favorable outcome, but act only on written clearance.

The written answer needs to cover:

- Ownership of a cash-pay virtual primary care practice.
- Seeing adult patients in Michigan outside CenterWell hours.
- Whether any age, payer, geography, diagnosis, or service-line exclusions apply.
- Whether you may operate a public website, publish content, run SEO, post on LinkedIn, or advertise.
- Whether you may target commercially insured, cash-pay, self-employed, or high-deductible adults.
- Whether you must avoid seniors, Medicare Advantage members, CenterWell patients, CenterWell referral sources, or CenterWell employees.
- Whether any medical director, advisory, protocol review, or consulting work is prohibited.
- Non-solicitation boundaries for patients, coworkers, APPs, vendors, and business partners.
- Use of employer time, devices, records, data, templates, and clinical materials.

If COI does not clear, do not route around it. Keep Present Health MD dormant or revisit the employment situation. A side practice that threatens the W-2 base is not a business win.

## The Constraint That Shapes Everything

You have one full-time job: CenterWell, 9-5 Monday through Friday. The practice must fit around that reality.

A realistic build budget is 12-18 reliable hours/week. A 20-25 hour week may happen during a push, but it should not be the operating assumption for months at a time.

Evening and weekend access can be a feature, not an apology:

- It fits working adults.
- It differentiates from traditional 9-5 primary care.
- It keeps the practice compatible with your W-2 job.

But after-hours-only care is not urgent care and not 24/7 coverage. The practice needs explicit boundaries:

- Stated response-time policy.
- No emergency care.
- No expectation of same-hour response.
- Clear escalation instructions.
- Backup plan for illness, vacation, travel, and family emergencies.

Visit capacity is not the main bottleneck. At steady state, most members may only need 2-4 visits/year. The binding constraint is message volume, refill/admin work, lab follow-up, care coordination, documentation, and context switching after a full workday.

Do not cross each panel threshold without measuring the work:

- 10 members: prove the clinical and billing workflow.
- 25 members: measure messages/member/month and admin minutes/member/month.
- 50 members: decide whether the offer, response-time promise, and scope are sustainable.
- 100 members: budget part-time admin help if the practice is still growing.
- 150 members: revisit whether this can remain side-built or needs a larger transition plan.

## The Exit Math

Target: approximately $150,000 in sustained yearly practice profit before leaving CenterWell.

Define this carefully. "Profit" should mean owner cash available after operating expenses and before personal income tax. It is not the same as W-2 compensation because W-2 work also carries benefits, retirement contributions, disability protection, paid time off, and lower business volatility.

At $99/month, one individual member is $1,188/year gross.

Planning costs:

- Clinical EHR: often several hundred dollars/month depending on vendor and modules.
- Membership/billing platform if separate: around $100/month starting point plus payment fees.
- Malpractice for solo telehealth DPC in Michigan: planning range $8,000-$15,000/year, but quote the actual scope.
- Telehealth and secure messaging: use EHR-integrated tooling where possible.
- Stripe/payment fees: roughly 3%-5% of revenue depending on card/ACH mix and platform fees.
- Website hosting, domains, and admin SaaS: $200-$500/month.
- Accounting, legal, entity, DPC agreement, privacy/security review: $3,000-$8,000/year early, potentially more at setup.
- Marketing: $0-$1,000/month early; $1,000-$3,000/month only after one channel works.
- License/CME maintenance: keep Michigan active; evaluate inactive status or low-cost maintenance for other states.
- Part-time admin/practice support: likely needed before the full exit target, not after it.

A disciplined solo virtual DPC practice could plausibly run at 55%-70% operating margin before owner tax once stable. To produce $150,000 in annual owner cash, the practice likely needs $215,000-$275,000 in annual recurring revenue.

At $99/month, that is roughly 180-230 individual-equivalent members. Household plans can reduce the count if average revenue per household is higher.

Exit decision rule:

- Two consecutive quarters at or above $150,000 annualized owner cash.
- Six months of personal cash reserves.
- Health insurance, disability coverage, retirement plan, and malpractice/tail implications understood.
- Churn stable.
- Response-time promise being met.
- Founder time no longer consistently exceeding the plan without a deliberate transition decision.

Do not leave CenterWell on one good month. Also do not wait for perfect certainty if the practice is already profitable, growing, and the W-2 job has become the main bottleneck. The decision is financial, operational, and personal.

## Pricing

Recommended launch tiers:

- Individual founding membership: $99/month or $990/year.
- Two-adult household: $179/month or $1,790/year.
- Children: defer until pediatric workflows, consent, guardian access, vaccine scope, and red-flag SOPs are real.
- Non-member single visit: $149 one-time, with the full $149 credited across the first 3 paid membership months if the patient converts within 30 days.

No first-month-free offer. It cheapens the relationship and attracts the wrong churn pattern.

Important pricing criticism: $99/month is probably right for launch, but it may underprice physician continuity once the offer is proven. Treat it as founding-member pricing for the first 50-100 members. Preserve the option to raise new-member pricing to $119-$129/month later while keeping existing members grandfathered.

This pricing stays under the 2026 DPC/HSA monthly limits cited in IRS guidance: $150 for one individual and $300 for arrangements covering more than one individual, assuming the arrangement otherwise qualifies.

## Insurance, GLP-1s, Employer PEPM, Advisory

Insurance:

- Do not bill insurance in 2026 or 2027.
- Do not credential.
- Do not build RCM.
- Do not touch denials, prior authorization, claim edits, payer audits, or plan contracts while this is side-built.

GLP-1s:

- Manage weight and cardiometabolic risk clinically.
- Prescribe appropriate FDA-approved therapies when clinically indicated and within law and scope.
- Do not build the brand around compounded GLP-1 access.
- Do not let GLP-1 demand distort the primary care identity.

Employer PEPM:

- Not a 2026 sales motion.
- Not a 2027 sales motion unless one warm anchor employer appears organically and asks for it.
- Membership is the model.

Advisory:

- Not part of the active 2026 plan.
- Reconsider only if COI blocks patient care and a clearly permitted advisory path becomes the least bad financial option.
- Otherwise, every practice-building hour goes into clinical operations, member acquisition, and retention.

## Market Position

Do not compete with Amazon One Medical, PlushCare, Circle Medical, Galileo, or Sesame on price, convenience, or commodity visits. They win that game.

Compete on:

- A real, named family physician with continuity.
- Evening and weekend access for working adults.
- Cardiometabolic chronic-disease focus.
- Medication cleanup and refill continuity.
- Cash-pay, HSA-compatible structure.
- No insurance friction.
- Direct messaging with a stated response-time policy.

Initial target:

- Adults 30-64.
- Michigan only.
- Commercially insured, high-deductible, self-employed, contractor, small business, or no useful PCP relationship.
- People who value continuity over episodic urgent care.
- People with blood pressure, prediabetes/type 2 diabetes, lipids, thyroid, weight-management, medication-continuity, or care-navigation needs.

Avoid seniors and Medicare Advantage as an initial target unless COI explicitly clears it. The CenterWell overlap risk is not worth it.

The clean positioning:

"Evening and weekend virtual primary care with a real Michigan family physician. Cash-pay membership. No insurance runaround."

## Clinical Infrastructure

Non-negotiable before the first paid patient:

- One clinical EHR selected and configured.
- BAAs executed with every vendor touching PHI.
- Michigan malpractice bound for the actual scope.
- DPC membership agreement reviewed for Michigan law and clear that this is not insurance.
- Identity verification at intake.
- State/location attestation at every visit.
- Telehealth informed consent.
- Controlled-substance prescribing policy.
- GLP-1 and weight-management policy.
- Lab workflow with Quest, Labcorp, or a direct-pay vendor.
- External record request workflow.
- Emergency escalation SOP.
- In-person referral rules.
- After-hours boundary and response-time policy.
- HIPAA-compliant messaging and video through EHR-integrated tooling where possible.
- Documentation, retention, and access-control policy.
- Backup coverage plan for illness, vacation, and emergencies.

EHR decision needs one correction: Hint Core and Elation are not interchangeable categories. Hint Core is primarily a direct-care business platform for membership administration, billing, and payments. Elation is a clinical EHR. If Hint is selected, confirm whether Hint Clinical meets the full clinical charting need or pair Hint Core with a real EHR such as Elation, Cerbo, Akute, or another DPC-capable chart.

The rule is not "one vendor." The rule is:

- One clinical source of truth.
- One membership/billing source of truth.
- No duplicate charting.
- No PHI in the custom marketing app.

The custom app stays marketing infrastructure. The clinical chart never lives in Prisma.

## State Strategy

Launch in Michigan only for at least the first 12 months.

Single-state launch means:

- One malpractice scope.
- One main medical board framework.
- One controlled-substance registration strategy.
- One set of telemedicine rules and standard-of-care references.
- Lower CME and license-maintenance burden during build phase.

A second state should wait until Michigan has:

- Reliable member growth.
- Stable retention.
- Manageable message volume.
- No recurring operational fire drills.
- Founder headroom or admin support.

Likely second-state candidates remain Florida, Texas, Arizona, or North Carolina, but only if demand and malpractice terms justify it.

For other licenses, evaluate cheap maintenance vs inactive status as renewals come up. Preserve optionality where cheap. Do not let the license map become the roadmap.

- COI-safe public positioning.
- Pricing cleanup.

- EHR handoff.
- Launch-state clarity.
- Non-PHI acquisition.

## 90-Day Roadmap

### Weeks 1-2: Groundwork

1. Confirm COI clearance in writing. Until it lands, keep the public site private or clearly prelaunch.
2. Book demos/workflow trials for Elation, Cerbo, Akute, and Hint. Decide clinical EHR first, then membership/billing.
3. Request malpractice quotes from 3 carriers for solo telehealth DPC in Michigan.
4. Confirm operating entity, registered agent, business bank account, bookkeeping, tax handling, and DPC agreement review.
5. Reduce the website to COI-safe positioning and a non-PHI waitlist.
6. Run 10 non-clinical fit interviews from your Michigan network. Ask what they would pay for, what they use now, what would make them cancel, and whether $99/month feels obvious or marginal.

### Weeks 3-6: Infrastructure Build

1. EHR configured: schedule templates, intake forms, telehealth consent, controlled-substance policy, GLP-1 policy, charting templates, secure messaging, video workflow.
2. Membership/billing configured through the EHR or direct-care billing platform.
3. Lab workflow chosen.
4. Emergency escalation SOP written, including chest pain, neurologic symptoms, suicidality, severe dyspnea, hypertensive emergency, anaphylaxis, and severe abdominal pain.
5. Michigan urgent care, ED, and specialist referral list compiled.
6. Telehealth workflow tested end-to-end with a friendly tester, not a patient.
7. Malpractice bound before any clinical care.
8. Marketing site updated to "launching" or "now accepting Michigan members" only if COI and malpractice are both done.

### Weeks 7-12: Soft Launch

1. Open to 5-10 paid founding members from your personal Michigan network.
2. Use real money, real EHR, real consent, real documentation.
3. No comps and no casual curbside care.
4. Time-track every visit, message, refill, lab follow-up, and admin task.
5. Fix what breaks.
6. Document repeatable workflows.
7. Collect testimonials or case stories only with explicit written consent and only if compliant.
8. Pick one acquisition channel: cardiometabolic SEO, LinkedIn under your real name, or one local Michigan referral source. Do not run multiple channels until one works.

End-of-90-days target:

- COI clear in writing.
- EHR and membership/billing stack live.
- Malpractice bound.
- Website honest.
- 5-15 paying members.
- Workflow issues visible and being fixed.

Base case by May 2027:

- 30-60 paying members.
- $36,000-$71,000 ARR.
- One organic acquisition channel producing 3-5 new members/month.
- Evening/weekend operations stable.
- Michigan only.
- One clinical chart.
- SOPs in production.
- No recurring response-time failures.

Upside case:

- 80-120 paying members.
- $95,000-$143,000 ARR.
- Acquisition channel producing 8-12 new members/month.
- Part-time admin support evaluated or hired.
- Second state evaluated only if Michigan is clearly stable.

Off-track signals:

- Fewer than 25 members at month 12 despite consistent effort.
- More than 20-25 hours/week required and still falling behind.
- Message response promises missed repeatedly.
- Patients want a different service than the one being sold.
- Churn indicates members do not perceive ongoing value after the first clinical need is handled.

## Path To $150K Profit And CenterWell Exit

A realistic side-built trajectory:

- Month 12: 30-100 members.
- Month 18: 70-150 members.
- Month 24: 120-190 members.
- Month 30-42: 180-230 members if acquisition, retention, and operations work.

The fastest path is not more features. It is:

- Keep the scope narrow.
- Master one acquisition channel.
- Retain members through responsiveness and continuity.
- Raise pricing for new members once proof exists.
- Hire admin help before admin work starts degrading care.
- Add a second clinician only after the practice cash-flows it and you know exactly what work is being delegated.

Levers that pull the exit closer:

- Household plan adoption.
- Low churn.
- Consistent message response.
- Strong founder reputation in Michigan.
- Organic referrals.
- Price increase for future cohorts.

Levers that push it out:

- Multi-state launch.
- Employer pilots.
- Insurance billing.
- GLP-1 storefront positioning.
- Building tech instead of recruiting members.
- Underpriced promotions.
- No backup plan for illness, vacation, or family emergencies.
